# Documentation

Apply this guidance to technical documents, design documents, and work logs.

## Write for the intended readers

- Supply the context readers need without relying on the editing conversation. Introduce relevant systems, terms, and constraints where they become necessary.
- Keep the document within its stated purpose and scope. Preserve an existing or requested format, and improve the flow within it.
- Include information that belongs in the document. Keep explanations of the editing process in the accompanying response.

## Match the document's purpose

- In technical documents, explain behavior, interfaces, prerequisites, and usage as needed for readers to understand or operate the system. Place examples and limitations beside the behavior they clarify.
- In design documents, connect the problem and constraints to the proposed approach. Explain component relationships and the reasons for consequential choices, including relevant alternatives, tradeoffs, and unresolved decisions. Distinguish proposals from implemented behavior.
- In work logs, preserve the sequence of events or the requested grouping. Record actions, outcomes, current status, and unresolved work with enough context for readers to follow progress. Distinguish completed work from plans and observations from interpretations.
